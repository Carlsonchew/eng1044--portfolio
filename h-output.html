The Positive Effects of Gamification on Students' Online Learning Platforms Final ▶
~2,375 words Prepared by: Alex Yeo Wei Onn (22016398), Chew Ker Zhen (24032971), Kyal Sinn Ye Htut (25013566) Prepared for: Ms. Siti Zuraiyni Yassin

1.0 Introduction

Gamification in education is similar to sugar in a child's diet, extremely appealing in the short term, but counterproductive if overused. The concept of gamification in education was proposed to increase its appeal. According to Rivera and Garden (2021), it's worth noting that although this concept is relatively new, teachers have been using it for years. In fact, for many years, excellent educators have been using gamification such as teaching strategies to enhance student engagement, such as incorporating elements of challenges, rules, and goals. Therefore, gamification is not a new concept. Although the term "gamification" is relatively new, its theoretical framework provides valuable support for learning design.

Nguyen-Viet and Doan Ngoc Minh (2025) said that gamification significantly impacts learners' motivation and enhances creativity, critical thinking, and communication skills. It can also improve students' academic performance. However, according to the research (Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022) we can determine that gamification itself can become a new distraction in learning. Gamification abuse occurs when students become overly engrossed in gamification and lose focus on learning. This abuse wastes students' valuable time and negatively impacts their learning efficiency. Nevertheless, gamification features are still more beneficial than harmful to online learning platforms. This article will start from self-regulated learning, increasing motivations and sparks discuss the effect of gamification features on students' motivation in online learning platforms.

2.0 Sparked Interest

The first argument is that incorporating gamification, interactivity, and culture-related content into online courses can enhance students' learning interest. Gamification can also stimulate student interest, reduce the tedium of learning, and thus increase learning efficiency. Astuti et al. (2025) dividing 120 students into experimental and control groups and employing quasi-experimental quantitative methods, an in-depth analysis of four indicators of learning interest (attention, relevance, confidence, and satisfaction) revealed that all these indicators showed a continuous upward trend. Among them, the increase in attention was the most significant, rising from 62.35 to 84.67 in the experimental group. Astuti et al. (2025) confirmed the significant impact of gamified media through multiple statistical tests.

However, Hadi Mogavi et al. (2022) found that the overuse of gamification mechanics can reduce users' interest in learning. After excessive gamification, learning will naturally become less enjoyable. Sometimes, students become so engrossed in it that they see real learning as an obstacle to their success in gamified learning. Hadi Mogavi et al. (2022) stated that more and more learning apps like Duolingo are using gamification (e.g., badges, points, and leaderboards) to enhance user learning. Through content analysis of nine years of data from the Duolingo forum and semi-structured interviews with 15 Duolingo users worldwide, Hadi Mogavi et al. (2022) found that some users felt Duolingo's gamification mechanisms had become more like a manipulative task, rather than a learning activity driven by personal interest. The findings suggest that users who become overly engrossed in competition (especially in learning environments) may become addicted and deviate from the true purpose of learning.

Nevertheless, gamified learning is still effective. Mat Zin & Md Osman (2026) said that gamified learning can make lessons more challenging, fun, and interactive, and students engage in learning when they use digital tools that are perceived as interesting and engaging. Gamification features can increase student interest, reduce boredom, and create fun learning experiences for students. Students who enjoy online learning are more likely to continue participating even when faced with challenging tasks. Indirectly, the experience may influence students' perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness of a platform; a pleasant experience makes the system feel easier to use and more valuable, thereby enhancing student acceptance and interest. Mat Zin & Md Osman (2026) advanced the theoretical development by linking the Technology Acceptance Model and Self-Determination Theory with outcomes. This model suggests creating a pleasant learning environment conducive to ability development. In summary, integrating gamification, interactivity, and culturally relevant content into online courses can increase students' learning interest and motivate them to learn more, thereby improving efficiency.

3.0 Increases Motivation

Moreover, the existing literature revealed that gamification plays an imperative role in motivating students in the online learning platforms. The adoption of gamification elements in education makes learning more interactive, fun and rewarding and motivates students to learn actively and participate in the learning process. First, Khaldi et al. (2023) conducted a systematic review of 90 studies to conclude that the major gamification elements that are commonly integrated into e-learning systems in higher education are points, badges, leaderboards, levels and feedback as these play an effective role in achieving learner interest and participation. The authors also noted that the recent studies feature challenges and storytelling elements to sustain learners' interest beyond just what can be given through reward systems. Challenge elements, in particular, are becoming increasingly common in recent studies. Gamification also builds the intrinsic motivation of students, beyond increasing participation. The findings suggest that the combinations of traditional game elements with deeper design features, gamification elements can maintain students' motivation over a longer period while encouraging active participation and sustained engagement. Second, in a systematic research and meta-analysis that examined 31 studies, Li, Hew and Du (2024) have concluded that gamification generates a significant and positive effect on intrinsic motivation and students' perceptions of autonomy and relatedness. Based on their study findings, meaningful choices, a feeling of social connection and a sense of ownership in learning are important to motivate students to participate in online learning activities.

In the study, however, Li, Hew and Du (2024) also discovered that the impact of gamification on perceived competence is minimal and that game elements need to be created appropriately, not as a means of giving positive reinforcement. Specifically, they discovered that some very common elements can be counterproductive to the goal of motivating students. Public absolute leaderboards, for instance, were said to cause students discomfort and generate social pressure about themselves when they were low on the list, and thus had a negative effect on their sense of motivation and achievements. Similarly, rewards such as badges and points were seen as controlling, rather than as a true reflection of progress, students experienced a loss of intrinsic motivation and diminished sense of autonomy. The limited impact was attributed by the researchers to an inappropriate level of challenge in gamified tasks and students' lack of knowledge of how the elements of gamification worked, which hindered students in enjoying a feeling of mastery and motivation. From these findings, it is inferred that gamification by inserting game elements into a course is inadequate, thus, it is necessary to be aware of the student's psychological needs so that the gamification can be effective in stimulating the students' motivation.

To address the shortfall of purposeless gamification design highlighted above, Rivera and Garden (2021) proposed a key component of gamification is to have intentional lesson design, and not just insert the game elements into a course. They offered the Framework of the Gamification for Student Engagement that was able to guide teachers to systematically select game attributes based on the specific engagement outcomes they hope to obtain. It provides a balance between the theoretical knowledge and application to create learning activities with gamification, making it possible to provide behavioural, cognitive and affective involvement rather than superficial involvement. This suggests that students' motivation is increased and strengthened when gamification is purposefully designed to support meaningful learning experiences rather than relying solely on rewards or competition. Overall, these research papers prove that if gamification features are designed appropriately, they can positively affect online learner motivation if it is compatible with the learner's psychological needs, boost active participation in the learning process, and match the learning goals with the game elements.

4.0 Self-Regulated Learning

Therefore, this paper argues that well designed gamification features positively affect students' motivation in online learning platforms by promoting self-regulated learning, increasing intrinsic motivation, and sparking greater interest in learning. The students' abilities to plan, monitor, evaluate and control their own learning behaviours in order to achieve their own academic goals is called Self-Regulated Learning (SRL). Gamification can help students regulate their own learning by increasing the intrinsic motivation given that it is properly designed and paired with the right supporting tools. As gamification has increasingly become popular with the rise of online learning, students are encouraged to take greater responsibilities for their own learning.

A research regarding thoughtful designs on gamification promoting students' self-regulated learning has been done to provide a systematic review. Mursalin et al. (2025) conducted a systematic review of 30 studies published between 2010 and 2022 and found that properly designed gamification system gradually improves students' enthusiasm and engagement across different educational levels. The review showed positive impact on motivation with most studies showing improvement of 20 to 35% when game features are integrated into teaching. Rather than relying only on ranks and points, the most effective learning environments incorporate structural feedback, progressive missions and collaborative activities that were closely aligned with learning objectives. These elements encourage students to monitor their own progress because continuous feedback allows the students to adjust their own learning pace, strategies and evaluate whether they are meeting their own goals, all of which are the essence of self-regulated learning. As a result, gamification system serve not only as a motivational tool but also a vital feature that supports the development of self-regulated learning by satisfying the students' needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness.

Similarly, Nguyen-Viet and Doan Ngoc Minh (2025) provided evidence supporting the relationship between gamification and self-regulated learning. Their study analysed data collected from 659 Vietnamese university students and examined the effects of achievement, immersion, and social interaction on learning effectiveness. The researchers found that self-regulation acts as a moderating variable in the relationship between intrinsic motivation and learning effectiveness. Students who were able to plan better, regulate their learning and monitor their own progress gained greater benefits from gamified learning environments than students with weaker self-regulating abilities. Based on these findings, the authors suggested that combining teaching strategies such as planning prompts, tracking tools and reflection activities with gamification system will help students increase motivation into achieving meaningful academic goals.

However, gamification does not always lead to beneficial outcomes. Nugroho et al. (2025) argued that poorly designed gamification can reduce rather than strengthen students' motivation and self-regulated learning. Their research found that relying excessively on external rewards, points accumulation and class ranking will lead to unnecessary stress, unhealthy competition among students and unwanted negative social hierarchy. Instead of focusing on understanding the learning materials, students will put all their focus on obtaining high ranking which ultimately weaken intrinsic motivation and lead to surface level learning.

Therefore, the effectiveness of gamification solely depends on thoughtful design which focus on aligning with learning objectives, provide adaptive feedback and encourage learner autonomy and self-monitoring. With all the right objectives in designs, gamification can become highly effective strategy for developing self-regulated learners. Although poorly designed gamification may create stress, unhealthy competition, and excessive focus on rewards, these negative effects do not mean that gamification itself is ineffective. Instead, they show that its success depends on whether the game features are meaningfully connected to learning objectives and supported by feedback, planning tools, and opportunities for reflection. Therefore, when thoughtfully designed, gamification can strengthen students' self-regulated learning by helping them stay motivated, monitor their progress, and take greater responsibility for achieving their academic goals.

5.0 Conclusion

In conclusion, gamification has mostly positive effects on students' online learning because it sparks interests of students, increases motivation, and enhances self-regulated learning. First, it was shown that gamification played a role in sparking students' interest in learning. Astuti et al. (2025) confirmed the significant impact of gamified media through multiple statistical tests. Mat Zin & Md Osman (2026) said that gamification features can increase student interest, reduce boredom, and create fun learning experiences for students. Students who enjoy online learning are more likely to continue participating even when faced with challenging tasks. Meanwhile, gamification is recognized as having its drawbacks in literature. Overuse of gamification mechanics, however, may have a negative effect and might result in diminished students' actual interest in the learning content and sometimes even in favouring the game elements over the learning content itself (Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022). However, the concern was challenged by the findings which showed that when game elements are used in moderation and tailored to fit students' psychological needs, they can still be effective motivation tools, namely by Mat Zin and Md Osman (2026) and by Astuti et al. (2025) who both stated that gamified, interactive and culturally relevant digital learning tools can still improve student engagement and enjoyment in learning, and that the danger of using gamification reducing the quality of learning is not on the use of game elements, but on the manner in which they are used.

This research shows that integrating gamification, interactivity, and culturally relevant content into online courses can increase students' learning interest and motivate them to learn more, thereby improving efficiency. Second, it was demonstrated that gamification directly contributed to enhancing student motivation and involvement. According to Khaldi et al. (2023), the most popular components that are widely used in higher education e-learning systems are points, badges, leaderboards, and feedback, while Li, Hew and Du (2024) found that gamification can positively influence intrinsic motivation, relatedness and autonomy perceptions, and that its motivation effects on competence perceptions are rather limited. Rivera and Garden (2021) further emphasized that all these benefits can only be realized when instructional design is intentional, which is achieved through their Gamification for Student Engagement Framework. Third, the study identified that gamification facilitated the SRL mechanism by creating an intrinsic motivation, autonomy, and relatedness for the students. Features like achievement systems and social interactions promote learners' autonomy in studying as emphasized by Nguyen-Viet and Doan Ngoc Minh (2025), and when learners' needs for autonomy and competence are satisfied, they can manage their learning behaviors more effectively on their own, as found by Li, Hew, and Du (2024) and Astuti et al. (2025).

Finally, this paper concludes that gamification is a strong tool to boost students' online learning specifically in the aspect of sparking interests, increases motivation and enhances self-regulated learning. As emphasized by Rivera and Garden (2021), the effectiveness of gamification depends not on the mere presence of game elements, but on the intentionality behind their instructional design.
