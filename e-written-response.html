A Brief Response on the Selected Articles Concerning the Interest Sparked by Gamification Among Students

The fast pace of technological advancements has led to more use of online learning platforms. The use of such platforms gives students flexibility and convenience because they can study from anywhere at any time by using electronic devices. To this day, online learning platforms have become increasingly important due to our increased dependence on education through digital means. Despite all the benefits of online learning platforms, student engagement and enthusiasm present major problems.

The importance of motivation cannot be undermined in terms of achieving success through studies. Students who are highly motivated are most likely to take an active part in their study process and produce successful results as well. Nevertheless, online learning does not ensure direct communication, and due to the nature of this type of education, students tend to become less engaged in the learning process.

To resolve such issues, several educational institutions and even websites devoted to online education have integrated elements of gamification into their system. Gamification in education can be defined as the integration of game mechanics, aesthetics, and game thinking to engage, motivate, and educate. Gamification in education means the implementation of game-design elements outside of the game context. Applying gamification elements such as points, badges, leaderboards, and challenges in learning environments can enhance students' intrinsic learning abilities and motivation (Astuti et al., 2025).

Though gamification may enhance the attractiveness of online learning, make the students more interested and engaged in the learning process, hence making them motivated enough to spend more time on learning tasks, gamification does not come without its own possible disadvantages. A major concern is that gamification itself may become a new distraction in learning. Gamification abuse occurs when students become overly engrossed in it, thus affecting their learning (Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022). Negative effects can also occur when students focus excessively on rewards without receiving them.

Consequently, the analysis of the effect of gamification elements in the online learning environment is essential. This study intends to investigate the effect of gamification elements on students' motivation to learn through the online learning environment and identify whether gamification elements make learning processes more engaging and motivating for students.

References

A Written Response on Selected Articles Concerning the Increase of Motivations Among Students Through Gamification

The issue of students' decreasing motivation in academic settings has become a growing concern for education researchers. Gamification, or the use of gameplay elements in non-game contexts, is a possible solution for engaging students in education. Specifically, Li et al. (2024) argue that students' intrinsic motivation in school-related activities tends to decline over the school year because their basic psychological needs are not sufficiently satisfied in a traditional educational environment. This written response is based on the two selected articles on how gamification can positively increase student motivations.

The first article is by Li et al. (2024), who performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of the effect of gamification on the intrinsic motivation of students in 35 independent interventions with 2500 participants involved in the study. The findings of the research demonstrate that gamified learning has a small but significant positive effect on intrinsic motivation (Hedges' g = 0.257, 95% CI [0.043, 0.471], p = .019). Particularly, it has positive and significant effects on students' perceived autonomy (Hedges' g = 0.638, 95% CI [0.139, 1.136], p = .012) and relatedness (Hedges' g = 1.776, 95% CI [0.737, 2.814], p = .001), but not much on competence (Li et al., 2024). The article overall showed that gamification does elevate the motivation of students with statistical evidence. Li et al. (2024) use self-determination theory (SDT) as the underlying framework for their study, which states that motivation increases when an activity satisfies three psychological aspects: competence (feeling of mastery) autonomy (a sense of choice and control over one's actions), and relatedness (a sense of connection with others). By using this framework, we can examine the conditions that make gamification effective in increasing student motivation with a theoretically based lens. This article is particularly relevant to our research project as it provides statistical evidence that gamification positively affects student motivation.

The next article is by Khaldi et al. (2023), who employed a systematic literature review of ninety papers on gamification in e-learning in higher education. According to Khaldi et al. (2023), the researchers found that points, badges, and leaderboards are the most widely used gamification elements used within the e-learning systems offered in higher education along with levels and feedback. The critical review found that most applied gamification research is non-theoretical, since only nine of the thirty-nine empirical studies adopted a motivational or theoretical framework to guide their design process (Khaldi et al., 2023). The authors observe that the theoretical and applied work is a gap that designers do not have a sufficient rationale for choosing or combining particular game elements (Khaldi et al., 2023), which risks producing gamified systems that do not meaningfully increase student motivations. This article is relevant to our research project because it highlights that improving student motivation through gamification requires not just adding game elements but selecting and combining them according to a sound motivational framework.

In conclusion, both articles are relevant to our research project as they offer some insights into how gamification can help in increasing student motivation. I plan to use the first article by Li et al. (2024) to strengthen my argument on the connection between gamification and students' motivation, and how gamified learning can positively increase their motivation. The second article by Khaldi et al. (2023) will be used to argue that gamification's potential to increase student motivation, as shown by Li et al. (2024), is only realized when designers deliberately select and combine game elements based on a motivational theory, rather than simply adding popular elements. I will look for more articles that would discuss specific gamification design frameworks and the impact gamification has on students and learning outcomes.

Li, L., Khe Foon Hew, & Du, J. (2024). Gamification enhances student intrinsic motivation, perceptions of autonomy and relatedness, but minimal impact on competency: a meta-analysis and systematic review. Educational Technology Research and Development, 72. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7

A Short Response on Selective Articles Concerning the Students' Self-regulated Learning through Gamification

As online learning has become widespread, almost all schools are now heavily implementing gamification features by using platforms such as Kahoot and Quizzes (Nugroho et al., 2025). According to Luarn et al. (2024), the integration of gamification has been shown to improve learning effectiveness by encouraging intrinsic motivation, which refers to engaging in activities for personal satisfaction rather than external rewards. Although previous studies have examined self-regulation in learning, most have treated it as an independent variable due to the limited research on its role (Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025). Therefore, this study aims to explore how self-regulation influences the relationship between gamification and learning for students.

According to Luarn et al. (2024), students become more motivated when they participate in gamified activities for enjoyment. This experience is closely linked to autonomy, which is one's ability to make choices and exercise personal freedom in decision-making (Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025). In gamified learning, students can express themselves freely, allowing them to learn at their own pace. As a result, their performance improves with stronger and more positive attitudes toward learning.

Furthermore, Nguyen-Viet and Doan Ngoc Minh (2025) analyzed data from 658 Vietnamese students to examine the effects of immersion and achievement in gamified learning. Students with stronger self-regulation skills gained more benefits from gamified online learning than other students. Since self-regulation involves managing thoughts, emotions and actions, it plays an important role in academic and personal development (Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025). Therefore, gamified learning that integrates self-regulated learning strategies can lead to better educational outcomes.

Although gamification can improve engagement, some studies have found a few consequences as well, such as competitive pressure and heavy emphasis on reward leading to greater focus on earning points than actually understanding the core material (Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025). While Luarn et al. (2024) emphasize the benefits of autonomy, Nugroho et al. (2025) highlight that poorly designed gamification systems may reduce these benefits. This suggests that the effectiveness of gamification depends not only on the classroom game elements but also on how they are implemented and how closely students can relate in cultural context. Similarly, Nugroho et al. (2025) also found that poorly designed gamification systems can become distracting and stressful, which may potentially lower students' self-esteem and motivation over time.

In conclusion, gamification has great potential to improve student motivation and learning effectiveness by supporting their self-regulation. However, its success depends on proper implementation by adopting local cultural and environmental contexts that can give comprehensive understanding of core materials to students rather than meaningless points that lead to competitions. By focusing on the psychological needs of the students, gamification can be implemented as a powerful tool for academic success.

References

Nguyen-Viet, B., & Doan Ngoc Minh, H. (2025). How Gamification Enhances Learning Effectiveness Through Blended Learning and Intrinsic Motivation: The Moderating Effects of Self-Regulation. Sage Open, 15(4). https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440251385846

Nugroho, M. A., Salma, A., Pradipa, A. R., & Putra, E. P. (2025). Exploring the Negative Effects of Gamification on Students: A PRISMA-Based Systematic Literature Review. 2025 International Conference on Computer and Applications (ICCA), 1–7. https://doi.org/10.1109/icca66035.2025.11431021

Luarn, P., Chen, C.-C., & Chiu, Y.-P. (2023). Enhancing intrinsic learning motivation through gamification: A self-determination theory perspective. The International Journal of Information and Learning Technology, 40(5), 413–424.
