Working Title

The Positive Effects of Gamification on Students' Online Learning Platforms

Thesis Statement

Well-designed gamification features positively affect students' motivation in online learning platforms by sparking greater interest in learning, increasing intrinsic motivation, and strengthening self-regulated learning (Astuti et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025).

Outline Table

Full outline mapped from Introduction → Body (3 main ideas) → Conclusion
Introduction Hook: Gamification in education is similar to sugar in a child's diet: it can make learning highly appealing, but it may become counterproductive when it is overused or poorly designed (Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022).

Thesis Statement: Well-designed gamification features positively affect students' motivation in online learning platforms by sparking greater interest in learning, increasing intrinsic motivation, and strengthening self-regulated learning (Astuti et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025).
Body Main Point 1: Sparked Interest Main Point 2: Increased Motivation Main Point 3: Self-Regulated Learning
Topic sentence: Gamification, interactivity, and culturally relevant content can spark students' interest by making online learning more enjoyable, engaging, and less repetitive (Astuti et al., 2025; Mat Zin & Md Osman, 2026). Topic sentence: Gamification increases students' motivation by making online learning more interactive, rewarding, and psychologically engaging (Khaldi et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024). Topic sentence: Well-designed gamification supports self-regulated learning by helping students plan, monitor, evaluate, and control their own learning progress (Mursalin et al., 2025; Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025).
Supporting point 1: Astuti et al. studied 120 students and found that gamified digital learning media improved attention, relevance, confidence, and satisfaction. Students' attention scores increased from 62.35 to 84.67 in the experimental group (Astuti et al., 2025). Supporting point 1: A systematic review of 90 studies found that points, badges, leaderboards, levels, feedback, challenges, and storytelling can increase students' participation and maintain their engagement in online learning (Khaldi et al., 2023). Supporting point 1: Structured feedback, progressive missions, and collaborative activities allow students to monitor their progress, adjust their learning pace, and evaluate whether they are achieving their academic goals (Mursalin et al., 2025).
Supporting point 2: Enjoyable and interactive digital learning tools reduce boredom and make online learning platforms appear easier, more useful, and more valuable to students, encouraging continued participation in challenging tasks (Mat Zin & Md Osman, 2026). Supporting point 2: A meta-analysis of 31 studies found that gamification positively affects intrinsic motivation and students' perceptions of autonomy and relatedness by providing meaningful choices, social connection, and ownership of learning (Li et al., 2024). Supporting point 2: A study involving 659 Vietnamese university students found that learners with stronger planning, monitoring, and self-regulation abilities gained greater benefits from gamified learning environments (Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025).
Counter argument: Excessive gamification may reduce genuine interest because students may become more concerned with collecting points, badges, streaks, or leaderboard positions than understanding the learning content (Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022). Counter argument: Public leaderboards and controlling rewards may cause social pressure and discomfort, especially among students who rank poorly, while excessive reliance on rewards may weaken intrinsic motivation and autonomy (Li et al., 2024). Counter argument: Poorly designed gamification may cause stress, unhealthy competition, negative social hierarchies, and surface-level learning when students focus mainly on rewards and rankings (Nugroho et al., 2025).
Refutation: The negative effects are mainly caused by the overuse or misuse of gamification. When game elements are used moderately and connected to learning activities, they can reduce boredom without replacing the educational content (Astuti et al., 2025; Mat Zin & Md Osman, 2026). Refutation: Gamification should not simply add points and rewards to a course. Game features must be intentionally selected according to specific behavioural, cognitive, and emotional engagement outcomes (Rivera & Garden, 2021). Refutation: The negative effects can be reduced by aligning game elements with learning objectives and including adaptive feedback, planning tools, progress tracking, learner autonomy, and reflection activities (Mursalin et al., 2025; Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025).
Section conclusion: Therefore, appropriately balanced gamification can spark students' interest, reduce boredom, and create a more enjoyable online learning experience (Astuti et al., 2025; Mat Zin & Md Osman, 2026). Section conclusion: Overall, gamification can strengthen students' intrinsic motivation and active participation when its features satisfy psychological needs and support meaningful learning goals (Li et al., 2024; Rivera & Garden, 2021). Section conclusion: Therefore, thoughtfully designed gamification can help students become more motivated, independent, and responsible for managing their own academic progress (Mursalin et al., 2025; Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025).
Conclusion Restated Thesis Statement: In conclusion, well-designed gamification has mostly positive effects on students' online learning because it sparks students' interest, increases intrinsic motivation, and strengthens self-regulated learning (Astuti et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025).
Summary of Main Points 1–3 Gamification makes online learning more enjoyable and interactive, reduces boredom, and encourages students to continue participating even when learning tasks become challenging (Astuti et al., 2025; Mat Zin & Md Osman, 2026). Game features such as challenges, feedback, achievements, meaningful choices, and social interaction can increase students' intrinsic motivation, autonomy, relatedness, and active engagement (Khaldi et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024). Gamification supports self-regulated learning by helping students set goals, track their progress, adjust their learning strategies, and take greater responsibility for achieving academic outcomes (Mursalin et al., 2025; Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025).
Final Statement Ultimately, the effectiveness of gamification depends not on the mere presence of points, badges, rewards, or leaderboards, but on whether these features are intentionally designed and meaningfully connected to students' learning objectives and psychological needs (Rivera & Garden, 2021).
