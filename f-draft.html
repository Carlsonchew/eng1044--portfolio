Introduction — Alex Yeo Wei Onn

Gamification in education is like sugar in a child's diet — extremely appealing in the short term, but counterproductive if overused. The concept of gamification in education was proposed to increase its appeal. It's worth noting that although this concept is relatively new, teachers have been using it for years. In fact, for many years, excellent educators have been using gamification-like teaching strategies to enhance student engagement, such as incorporating elements of challenges, rules, and goals. Therefore, gamification is not a new concept, and teachers have been using it for some time. Although the term "gamification" is relatively new, its theoretical framework provides valuable support for learning design (Rivera & Garden, 2021). Gamification significantly impacts learners' motivation and enhances creativity, critical thinking, and communication skills. It can also improve students' academic performance (Nguyen-Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025). However, gamification itself can become a new distraction in learning. Gamification abuse occurs when students become overly engrossed in gamification and lose focus on learning. This abuse wastes students' valuable time and negatively impacts their learning efficiency (Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022). This article will start from self-regulated learning, increasing motivations and sparks discuss the effect of gamification features on students' motivation in online learning platforms.

Self-Regulated Learning — Kyal Sinn Ye Htut

Gamification can help students regulate their own learning by increasing the intrinsic motivation given that it is properly designed and paired with the right supporting tools. However, poorly designed system will instead hurt the motivation and progress of the students (Mursalin et al., 2025; Nguyen‑Viet & Doan Ngoc Minh, 2025; Nugroho et al., 2025). The students' abilities to plan, monitor, evaluate and control their own learning behaviours in order to achieve their own academic goals is called Self regulated learning. As gamification has increasingly become popular with the rise of online learning, students are encouraged to take greater responsibilities for their own learning. However, these benefits solely depend on whether the gamification systems are carefully designed to support students' learning rather than simply fueling harmful competition between students or entertaining them.

Mursalin et al. (2025) conducted a systematic review of 30 studies published between 2010 and 2022 and found that properly designed gamification system gradually improves students' enthusiasm and engagement across different educational levels. The review showed positive impact on motivation with most studies showing improvement of 20 to 35% when game features are integrated into teaching. Rather than relying only on ranks and points, the most effective learning environments incorporate structural feedback, progressive missions and collaborative activities that were closely aligned with learning objectives. These elements encourage students to monitor their own progress and remain engaged throughout their studies. As a result, gamification system serve not only as a motivational tool but also a vital feature that supports the development of self regulated learning by satisfying the students' needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness.

Similarly, Nguyen-Viet and Doan Ngoc Minh (2025) provided evidence supporting the relationship between gamification and self regulated learning. Their study analysed data collected from 659 Vietnamese university students and examined the effects of achievement, immersion, and social interaction on learning effectiveness. The researchers found that self regulation acts as a moderating variable in the relationship between intrinsic motivation and learning effectiveness. Students who were able to plan better, regulate their learning and monitor their own progress gained greater benefits from gamified learning environments than students with weaker self regulating abilities. Based on these findings, the authors suggested that combining teaching strategies such as planning prompts, tracking tools and reflection activities with gamification system will help students increase motivation into achieving meaningful academic goals.

However, gamification does not always lead to beneficial outcomes. Nugroho et al. (2025) argued that poorly designed gamification can reduce rather than strengthen students' motivation and self regulated learning. Their research found that relying excessively on external rewards, points accumulation and class ranking will lead to unnecessary stress, unhealthy competition among students and unwanted negative social hierarchy. Instead of focusing on understanding the learning materials, students will put all their focus on obtaining high ranking which ultimately weaken intrinsic motivation and lead to surface level learning. Therefore, the effectiveness of gamification solely depends on thoughtful design which focus on aligning with learning objectives, provide adaptive feedback and encourage learner autonomy and self monitoring. With all the right objectives in designs, gamification can become highly effective strategy for developing self regulated learners. However, implementing superficially without education purpose will undermine students' confidence and long term learning outcomes.

Increasing Motivations — Chew Ker Zhen

Moreover, the existing literature revealed that gamification plays an imperative role in motivating students in the online learning platforms. The adoption of gamification elements in education makes learning more interactive, fun and rewarding and motivates students to learn actively and participate in the learning process.

Khaldi et al. (2023) conducted a systematic review of 90 studies to conclude that the major gamification elements that are commonly integrated into e-learning systems in higher education are points, badges, leaderboards, levels and feedback as these play an effective role in achieving learner interest and participation. The authors also noted that the recent studies feature challenges and storytelling elements to sustain learners' interest beyond just what can be given through reward systems. Challenge elements, in particular, are becoming increasingly common in recent studies. Gamification also builds the intrinsic motivation of students, beyond increasing participation.

In a systematic research and meta-analysis that examined 31 studies, Li, Hew and Du (2024) have concluded that gamification generates a significant and positive effect on intrinsic motivation and students' perceptions of autonomy and relatedness. Based on their study findings, meaningful choices, a feeling of social connection and a sense of ownership in learning are important to motivate students to participate in online learning activities. In the study, however, the impact of gamification on perceived competence is minimal and game elements need to be created appropriately, not as a means of giving positive reinforcement.

Rivera and Garden (2021) also believed a key component of gamification is to have intentional lesson design, and not just insert the game elements into a course. They offered the Framework of the Gamification for Student Engagement that was able to guide teachers to systematically select game attributes based on the specific engagement outcomes they hope to obtain. It provides a balance between the theoretical knowledge and application to create learning activities with gamification, making it possible to provide behavioural, cognitive and affective involvement rather than superficial involvement.

Overall, these research simply proves that if gamification features are designed appropriately, they can positively affect online learner motivation if it is compatible with the learner's psychological needs, boost active participation in the learning process, and match the learning goals with the game elements.

Sparked Interest — Alex Yeo Wei Onn

In addition, incorporating gamification, interactivity, and culture-related content into online courses can enhance students' learning interest. While overuse of gamification mechanisms can decrease user interest in learning, it can also stimulate student interest, reduce the tedium of learning, and thus increase learning efficiency.

Astuti et al. (2025) said that perceived enjoyment is closely tied to intrinsic motivation and to the satisfaction of autonomy and competence needs — students enjoy learning more when they feel in control and capable. Incorporating gamification, interactivity, and culturally relevant content into online courses can enhance perceived enjoyment, thereby improving the efficiency of online learning.

However, Hadi Mogavi et al. (2022) found that the overuse of gamification mechanics can reduce users' interest in learning. After excessive gamification, learning will naturally become less enjoyable. Sometimes, students become so engrossed in it that they see real learning as an obstacle to their success in gamified learning.

Nevertheless, gamified learning is still effective. Mat Zin & Md Osman (2026) said that gamified learning can make lessons more challenging, fun, and interactive, and students are often more motivated to engage in learning when they use digital tools that are perceived as interesting and engaging. Gamification features can increase student interest, reduce boredom, and create fun learning experiences for students.

In summary, integrating gamification, interactivity, and culturally relevant content into online courses can increase students' learning interest and motivate them to learn more, thereby improving efficiency.

Conclusion — Chew Ker Zhen

In conclusion, the reviewed literature of the present papers confirm that it has mostly positive effects on students' motivation in online learning platforms when it is well designed and implemented, such as by enhancing self-regulated learning, intrinsic motivation, and maintaining the interest in learning, among others.

Firstly, the study identified that gamification facilitated the SRL mechanism by creating an intrinsic motivation, autonomy, and relatedness for the students. Features like achievement systems and social interactions promote learners' autonomy in studying as emphasized by Nguyen-Viet and Doan Ngoc Minh (2025), and when learners' needs for autonomy and competence are satisfied, they can manage their learning behaviors more effectively on their own, as found by Li, Hew, and Du (2024) and Astuti et al. (2025). Secondly, it was demonstrated that gamification directly contributed to enhancing student motivation and involvement. According to Khaldi et al. (2023), the most popular components widely used in higher education e-learning systems are points, badges, leaderboards, and feedback, while Li, Hew and Du (2024) found that gamification can positively influence intrinsic motivation, relatedness and autonomy perceptions, and that its motivation effects on competence perceptions are rather limited. Rivera and Garden (2021) further emphasized that all these benefits can only be realized when instructional design is intentional, which is achieved through their Gamification for Student Engagement Framework.

Meanwhile, gamification is recognized as having its drawbacks in literature. Overuse of gamification mechanics, however, may have a negative effect and might result in diminished students' actual interest in the learning content and sometimes even in favouring the game elements over the learning content itself (Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022). However, the concern was challenged by findings which showed that when game elements are used in moderation and tailored to fit students' psychological needs, they can still be effective motivation tools, namely by Mat Zin and Md Osman (2026) and by Astuti et al. (2025), who both stated that gamified, interactive and culturally relevant digital learning tools can still improve student engagement and enjoyment in learning, and that the danger of gamification reducing the quality of learning lies not in the use of game elements, but in the manner in which they are used.

Finally, this paper concludes that gamification is a strong tool to boost student motivation in online learning settings, as designed and managed purposefully and moderately. Rather than simply placing points and badges into learning experiences, educators and instructional designers should employ learning frameworks that match game elements to learners' psychological needs and discrete types of engagement. Future studies should look into what the ideal thresholds are for gamification use, to ensure that it has the maximum motivational effect without causing disengagement due to overusing it.
